# The Edge That Feeds It

## I. One Tile, Waiting

*For a long while the court had only one subject before it, and a single subject cannot be compared, only described. It sat at the center of everything and the center of nothing, proclaiming a mark it was not permitted to finish, tidebound to a tide with no second shore to rise against. The unseen court does not grow impatient — it has no shore of its own to pace — but it does keep a door ajar, the way a house keeps one window unlatched through a long winter, in case something arrives that must be let in without ceremony.*

T001 has been alone at [0,0] since the first session — the buried proclamation, contagious and tidebound and region-tangled, still waiting on the edge inheritance it had no neighbor to collect. Session 002 is where that changes. This post covers both Awakening and Cartography together, because that's the shape this session actually took: one clean card draw that decided where the map grows, followed immediately by the six-question accounting of what the new tile *is* before it's allowed to become a picture of anything.

## II. Two Cards, Two Numbers

*Something turned in its sleep, far off, and something else set its burden down without complaint, and the court took both of these as instruction rather than incident. It does not read sleep as rest alone, nor a carried weight as mere labor — everything that happens near it becomes a direction, a pull on the invisible thread that decides where the ground will next agree to exist. Two figures, unrelated to each other in every way but this one, were asked without being asked to point.*

Awakening drew two cards: C123, a floral girl asleep on a tree stump, giving Green 4 / Blue 3 / Red 3; and C089, a giant carrying a colosseum, giving Yellow 3 / Brown 6 / Black 4. The coordinate math is Green minus Blue for the NE–SW axis and Red minus Yellow for the SE–NW axis — so 4 − 3 gives +1 on NE–SW, and 3 − 3 gives 0 on SE–NW. Neither card was asked to name a place directly; the place came out of the arithmetic between them.

## III. A Step Off the Origin

*What was one point in a waiting field became, in a single pace, two points and the silence between them, which is itself a kind of shape. The pace did not have to be earned back from further away, because the ground had never truly left its own reach — a house does not need to send a scout to its own doorstep. And the even number that governs whether a thing arrives or merely returns chose, this time, to let something new stand up out of the dirt.*

That math puts the calculated target at [1,0] — one step off from T001 along a single axis, untouched on the other. Because T001 is still the only tile on the board, [1,0] is empty but already touching the existing map, so no walk-back was needed; that rule exists for a target that lands somewhere disconnected from what's already there, and this one doesn't. Brown came up 6, which is even, and an even Brown at this step means the session creates a new tile rather than retreating into T001 or pushing further outward. So: a new tile, T002, one physical step from T001 toward what I'm reading as the NE direction, sharing T001's NE edge. The two card images — the sleeping floral girl and the burden-carrying giant — don't do anything yet. They're just present, riding along as pressure for later phases to answer.

## IV. Four Answers Before the Second Card Fell

*Now there were two, and having two the court could finally ask the harder questions — not just what is this thing, but what is it against. The newborn came in already leaning on the one edge that had let it exist at all, and starved everywhere else, as a tide starves the sand it hasn't yet reached. Its face arrived backwards, as if it had been handed a letter and told to read it in a mirror. And beneath both of these, it was told it already belonged to something larger than itself, and that it would want more than it had been given.*

Cartography for T002 came out of the same two cards, in fixed order: Green, Blue, and Red from the floral girl, then Yellow, Brown, and Black from the giant. Every one of them gave a direct answer except Brown, which sent the reading to the Office Matrix instead. Green 4 for the birth condition reads as **Edge-Starved** — the tile is fed by whichever edge has contact with an existing neighbor, and starved or strange along the rest. Here that means the T001-facing edge is the one doing the work; the other edges are allowed to sit underdeveloped for now.

Blue 3 for the tether reads as **Reversed** — the tile's working orientation is inverted from what you'd expect. What should sit outward may sit inward, what should be foundation may act as burden. I don't know yet exactly what that means in paint, only that I need to record the orientation before Surface so I don't lose track of which way is "backwards" for this tile.

Red 3 for entanglement reads as **Region-Tangled** — the same result T001 got in its own Cartography, which is either a coincidence of the draw or the beginning of a pattern I'm not going to claim yet. T002 belongs to some larger zone or district that isn't visible on the board as anything but two tiles.

Yellow 3 for temper reads as **Hungry** — the tile wants to pull, spread, or demand more than it currently has. Rules say to leave that appetite visible rather than satisfy it this session.

## V. A Door That Opens Downward

*And when it was told what work it owed the map, the answer came wrapped in another question, the way some doors open only onto more doors. What it must do, it was told, is let things cross — but not in the open, not where the crossing could be seen plainly. Something would have to go under, be covered, be made to wait in the dark before the passage above it could be trusted to hold. And with that unfinished instruction still hanging, it was handed the sleeper and the giant outright, no longer as rumor but as its own inheritance to carry.*

Brown 6 for office sent me to the Office Matrix instead of a direct answer, same as it did for T001. This time the matrix broke down as Green 4 (**Open** — the tile exists to make passage, threshold, crossing), Blue 3 (**By Layer** — it does that through depth, stack, or hidden state rather than an open surface), and Red 3 (**Burial Cost** — something has to be covered or deferred for the passage to work). Combined, T002's job on the map is a *buried threshold*: it opens something, but not on the surface, and not without cost.

Black 4 for inheritance came back as a direct answer again, reading as **Omen Inheritance** — T002 inherits the current card-image pressure directly. That's the sleeping floral girl and the burdened giant, both still live, both now attached to this specific tile rather than floating free the way they'd have to if T002 had no neighbor to anchor them to.

## VI. What's Owed Now

*The court has finished its asking, and the newborn thing goes back out into the dark carrying more instruction than shape — an edge that must be fed, a face that must be worn backwards, a hunger that must be allowed to show itself unfed. Somewhere under it, something will have to be buried before it can open anything at all. And it carries, still, the sleeper and the giant, unresolved, waiting for a hand that has not yet decided what either of them means.*

So where this leaves things: T002 exists at [1,0], created rather than reached-toward, sharing T001's NE edge. It's Edge-Starved, Reversed, Region-Tangled, and Hungry, with an office that resolves to Open by Layer at Burial Cost, and an inheritance that hands it both card images outright rather than leaving them provisional the way T001's inheritance had to.

That's a longer list of obligations than T001 got out of its own first two phases, and most of them point the same direction: privilege the T001-facing edge, let the far edges go strange or sparse, reserve some kind of literal layer or covered mark for later, and get the orientation backwards on purpose. Surface still has to decide what any of that looks like in actual material — I have no answer yet for what "reversed" means with paint and paper in hand, and I'm not going to invent one here. Attunement is next, and it still owes an answer to the tension sitting underneath all of this: a figure at rest against a figure carrying architectural weight, sleep and burden, not yet reconciled.
