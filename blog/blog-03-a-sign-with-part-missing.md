# A Sign With Part of Itself Missing

## I. What Cartography Decides

*Before the shape may be dressed, it must first be named before the unseen court. Not its colors — the court does not care for colors — but its standing among the things that already are and the things not yet born. Does it root, or does it drift on another's tide. Does it belong only to itself, or has some larger dominion already claimed it before it drew breath. Does it speak freely, or is its tongue bound by a law older than the speaking. These are the questions asked of every newborn thing, and they are asked before it is given a face to answer with.*

The second phase of a session is called Cartography, and it happens before anything gets painted, cut, or glued. Where Awakening decides *where* the map grows, Cartography decides *what kind of thing* is growing there — as a piece of the map-system, not yet as a picture. For a brand new tile, that means answering six questions about how it's born: how it emerges into the map, how firmly it's bound to its place, what larger thing it's already entangled with, what inner force it carries, what job it does for the map, and what it inherits from elsewhere.

Cartography isn't allowed to jump ahead into Surface or Inscription. No paint, no marks, no finished imagery — just the tile's standing as a map-object, recorded so that later phases know what they're building on top of.

## II. Four Answers for a Tile That Has No Neighbors Yet

*It came into the world already leaning past its own borders, as though somewhere else had called to it before it had finished arriving here. No solitary thing, this — it is claimed at birth by a dominion too vast yet to be seen, the way a single drop is already claimed by the sea it will join. And it does not come light. It comes gravid, close-packed, the way a held breath is packed, the way a fist closes around something it will not yet show. Four truths were laid on it before a single mark existed, and the unseen court gave none of them lightly.*

The first four answers came out of the robot ghost and the lion together. Green gave a `5`, which reads as contagious: the tile is born carrying a force that may bleed outward later. There's nothing to bleed onto yet — T001 has no neighbors — so for now this is a future-facing pressure rather than something happening at an edge today. Blue also gave a `5`, which reads as tidebound: the tile is bound to some other moving condition rather than sitting still on its own terms. With no coastline or route to bind to yet, the sensible read is that T001 is tidebound to the two cards themselves — tied to whatever the robot ghost and the lion turn out to mean as this project goes on. That's two `5`s out of the first two draws, which feels worth noting even though the rules don't ask me to make anything of it yet.

Red gave a `3`, region-tangled: T001 isn't a lone dot on the grid, it's already entangled with something larger — a territory or zone it belongs to before that territory has any other visible pieces. And yellow gave a `2`, dense: the tile's inner nature wants accumulation, crowding, layered material, not open space. That one's a direct instruction for later — Surface is going to need to build this tile up with actual bulk and texture, not leave it spare or thin.

## III. A Mark That Isn't Allowed to Show All of Itself

*Something in it wants to be seen. That much the court does not dispute — a voice is rising in it, straining toward a name spoken aloud, a sign held up to the light. But the same throat that would proclaim is also the hand that covers its own mouth mid-word. What is said will not be said whole. Half of it is led down into the dark grain of the thing and told to wait there, patient as buried treasure, for another hand, in another season, to lift the covering and let the rest of the sentence finish itself.*

The fifth question, office, needed a second draw because brown came up `6` — a trip to the Office Matrix rather than a direct answer. That matrix broke down into three more numbers: green `5` gave proclaim, meaning the tile exists to announce, signal, or reveal something; blue `5` gave by mark, meaning it does that specifically through a symbol, sign, label, or wound rather than through shape or structure; and red `3` gave burial cost, meaning that whatever this announcing mark turns out to be, part of it has to be covered, submerged, or made latent rather than shown in full.

Put together, T001's job on the map is a buried proclamation. It's going to carry a sign that wants to be read, and the rules are already insisting that the sign can't be fully legible yet — something about it gets covered, deferred, or handed off to a later phase or a future tile to finish revealing.

Black gave a `1` for inheritance, which reads as edge inheritance — T001 inherits something from a nearby or touching edge. There is no neighbor to inherit from yet, so this one has to sit as a provisional idea rather than an actual feature: T001's edges aren't just blank borders, they're already waiting on something that hasn't arrived.

## IV. What Surface and Inscription Now Owe

*The court has spoken, and the newborn thing is dismissed still faceless, carrying its sentence like a folded letter it cannot yet read. Two hands will open that letter later — one to give the thing weight and ground, one to give it a mouth. Until then it waits at the edge of its own becoming, dense with what it has not yet shown, bound to a tide that has not yet risen, proclaiming a name it is forbidden, for now, to finish saying.*

What's decided, in plain terms: T001 is contagious, tidebound, and region-tangled by its first three numbers, dense by its fourth, and its office — the job it performs for the map — resolves to proclaim by mark under a burial cost. Its inheritance is an edge inheritance it can't yet collect on, since there's no neighboring tile to inherit from. Six questions, six answers, and not one stroke of paint spent on any of them — that's the discipline of this phase, and it's a harder discipline to keep than it sounds, because I already want to know what the mark is going to be.

What that leaves for later: Surface owes this tile density — built-up, layered, crowded ground rather than open space — and it should leave two edges unresolved, since edge inheritance has nowhere to land until a neighbor exists to hand something across it. Inscription, whenever it arrives, owes T001 an actual mark: a sign, number, label, or wound that announces the tile's presence — but made so that part of it stays covered, submerged, or otherwise withheld. The robot ghost and the lion are still just pressures for now, not pictures. What T001 is allowed to say about itself, it isn't allowed to say all at once.
