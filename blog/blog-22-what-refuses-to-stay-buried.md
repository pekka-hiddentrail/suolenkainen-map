# What Refuses to Stay Buried

## I. Seven Tiles, Three Debts Still Owed

*The origin had stood at the center of everything for seven sessions, and in all that time it had only ever received — a bleed here, a hexagon there, symbols rewritten by younger ground that had learned to speak before it ever had to. It never once reached back. A question asked of it in the second session was still standing exactly where it had been left, patient the way old debts are patient, never quite forgotten and never quite paid. And then something came looking for it specifically, carrying its own oldest name like a key that had been missing a lock for seven turns.*

Session Seven closed with Mesa built and named, its own Quiet Cost still unpaid. Further back, a debt from Session Six never got paid either: The Monster's Contagious bleed should have reached Shoreline once the two turned out to be neighbors, but the relation wasn't recognized in time. And further back still, the map's oldest open question — whether AIKA's own warning-strip bleed into Ring ever really settled anything — has sat since Session Two, inherited by newer tiles in passing but never acted on by AIKA itself.

Seven tiles stand now: AIKA at the origin, Ring northeast, the Canyon north of AIKA and northwest of Ring, Mirror southwest of AIKA, The Monster west of Mirror, Shoreline touching four of them at once, Mesa alone north of the Canyon. Awakening starts from Mesa, the last tile targeted.

## II. The Reach That Finally Turned Around

*The reach landed at two doors this time, the way it once had before, and once again there was no need to walk anywhere at all — the ground was simply, immediately, already there. But an old instruction, written for a very different situation, was asked a question it had never faced: what does one step further even mean, when no steps were ever taken to begin with? An answer was found, plain and honest, and it sent the reach not outward to something new, but backward, for the very first time, to the place everything else had started from.*

Awakening drew C101, Green power leaf, giving Green 3 / Blue 5 / Red 5, and C100, Zombie, giving Yellow 2 / Brown 5 / Black 1. NE-SW comes out to 3 minus 5, or −2; SE-NW comes out to 5 minus 2, or +3. From Mesa at [2,-2], that puts the raw target at [0,1].

[0,1] is empty but already touches two tiles at once — AIKA's NW neighbor and Ring's N neighbor — before any walk-back step, so per the already-touching rule, no stepping is needed; straight to Brown-parity. And here the map hit something new: every prior already-touching landing rolled an even Brown and simply became a new tile. This time Brown came up 5, odd — and the odd branch was written for the ordinary walk-back flow, assuming a direction of travel already established from real steps. No steps had been taken here at all.

The fix: work out the nearest reference line the same way the ordinary mechanism would, then take one real step further along it. [0,1] already sits exactly on such a line — the axis where the first coordinate is zero. One step further, NW, lands at [0,0]. Occupied. By AIKA.

For the first time this campaign, the target lands on an existing tile rather than an empty one. Session Eight becomes AIKA's own revisit — the first time Phase 2.2, the existing-tile Cartography redesigned as a causal chain, actually gets used for real.

## III. A Name Spoken, and an Old Cost Stirs

*A name was spoken that the tile had carried quietly since its very first breath, and hearing it said aloud again did not change what it meant — it only woke it. An old cost stirred in its grave rather than staying settled, the way the dead in that same card's own image are said to do. A scarred corner, touched twice now by younger, more talkative ground, was finally called what it had already, plainly become. And for the first time, something chose to walk back the way it had come, rather than simply wait, again, to be visited.*

Cartography ran the existing-tile procedure for the first time, instead of a new tile's birth questions. The six numbers read as Activation (Name Spoken: an existing keyword or omen gets invoked), Tile Effect (Content Change: a named feature gets added, renamed, or reassigned), Map Effect (Radiating: something concrete crosses into a neighbor), Constraint (Identity Locked: a founding trait can't be reinterpreted this session), Cost (Relational Cost: a bond to a neighbor becomes a hard obligation), and Carry-Forward (Attunement must engage directly with whatever Activation wakes).

Grounded in AIKA's own history, each result lands with weight. Name Spoken is the Zombie card itself: its name invokes K007, Burial Cost — AIKA's Office has always been to proclaim by mark, paid for by something buried or made latent, and a zombie is the literal image of a burial cost refusing to stay paid. The cost isn't renegotiated — Identity Locked forbids that — just stirred again. Content Change targets a feature quietly changing for three sessions without ever being properly named: the "bottom archaic-symbol area," rewritten in part by Mirror in Session Four and reached into by Shoreline's silver hexagon in Session Six. It gets a real name now — the archaic-symbol palimpsest — recognized as multiply-modified rather than one untouched zone things happen to land on.

Radiating is where the real turn happens: something concrete crosses back out of the palimpsest into Shoreline — the most recent tile to reach into it. That's AIKA finally answering from its own side, rather than leaving the question framed, as its own record has said, as something that "may get answered from Shoreline's side instead." Relational Cost makes the turn permanent: the bond stops being an open maybe and becomes a hard obligation on both records at once.

Identity Locked holds throughout — none of this touches what AIKA fundamentally is. Contagious, Tidebound, Region-Tangled, Dense, its Proclaim/By-Mark/Burial-Cost office, its Edge Inheritance all stand exactly as they always have. What changes is smaller and larger at once: one feature finally gets called what it's already become, and one of AIKA's oldest relationships loses the ability to just sit there forever. The debt waiting since Session Two finally has to move.

## IV. Six Results, One Grave to Open

*Every question the cards asked this time bent back toward the very same answer, as if for once the whole reading had only one thing left to say. A material was chosen for how convincingly it could look already dug up, rather than freshly placed — old before it had even been used. A color was crowned not because it belonged to the dead card alone, but because a shoreline far off had already been speaking in that same green tongue, unasked. And for the first time here, the demand was not to cover further, but to open what had only ever been closed — even while, in the same breath, some part of it was being sent away to somewhere else entirely.*

Attunement reused the same six numbers, remapped onto Echo, Matter, Mirror, Omen, Pressure, and Provision. Unusually, all six converge tightly on one thing — Cartography's own Carry-Forward demand that Attunement read and respond to the Burial Cost the Zombie card woke — rather than spreading across separate threads the way results usually do.

Echo came up Unresolved, and it picks the exact obligation Cartography already activated — the AIKA-Shoreline warning-strip question, now a hard Relational Cost bond — reinforcing it with a second, independent hook rather than reaching for something else.

Matter came up Acquire: something that reads as dug up rather than freshly made — rust tones, bone-white, aged paper — not needed this session, reserved for whenever the palimpsest needs further treatment. Mirror came up Prepared Mirror: a fragment of the Zombie card's own artwork, specifically its decayed, cracked texture rather than the whole image, kept as the actual visual guide for how the mark traveling to Shoreline should read.

Omen came up Hue: sickly, undead green — not just "the zombie's color," but the same register Shoreline's own emerald river-grooves already speak in, so whatever radiates outward can visually agree with what's already there rather than adding a fourth unrelated color.

Pressure came up Correction, read as Reveal something — the one result running against the grain of everything done at this corner so far. The palimpsest has only ever been covered, across three sessions: Mirror's rewrite, then Shoreline's hex-push, never once opened back up. This session demands the opposite — expose part of what's underneath, even while a separate piece is simultaneously sent outward. Covering and uncovering, in the same gesture.

Provision came up Mark: a blank marker prepared not as a generic reserve but specifically to carry whatever word or symbol ends up being the content of the response crossing to Shoreline, staying blank until Inscription decides.

Real material choices followed before Surface even started. Metallic paint went on the list for Acquire — unearthed, tarnished metal that also echoes Shoreline's own silver hexagon. Green tissue paper, already on hand, does double duty for Omen and Pressure at once: its translucency tints the surface sickly green while still letting a hint of what's underneath show through — one material solving both color and reveal. And for Mirror's reference, super glue worked into silk paper should produce a crackle effect sharper and less even than a slower crackle medium, better suited to decay than something neat.

## V. The Word Hidden Inside a Word

*A corridor was cut through the oldest ground, asking only to touch the lowest, least-examined stretch of it — not to remake the whole tile, only to decide which sliver of it this hour was allowed to reach. Its own name turned out to be hiding inside a longer word, the same way the thing it was meant to expose had been hiding inside something older for three sessions running. It reached further than it was asked to, spilling into two neighbors instead of the one it owed. And when it was finished, more of the old ground was covered than had been uncovered — an honest failure of the very thing it set out to do, left standing rather than smoothed over.*

Surface reused the same six numbers, remapped onto Ground, Substance, Application, Treatment, Structure, and Opening. There's no First Mark this time — that rule belongs to a tile's birth, and AIKA already has one from Session One.

Ground came up Divided, reading differently for a revisit than a birth: not a new foundation split into zones, but a division of where this session is even allowed to touch — AIKA's original Session One surface stays untouched, with everything happening inside one new zone. Structure came up Shape, giving that zone its geometry: a corridor running from the archaic-symbol palimpsest to the Shoreline-facing edge, the most literal shape for something that has to travel point to point, exactly what Radiating needs. Application came up Edgeward, giving the corridor a direction: starting at the palimpsest and working outward, matching the way the pressure actually needs to travel.

Substance came up Strange, and rather than needing something new, it was already on the tile: the real fan-brush, epoxy-glued to AIKA since Session One, is exactly "not normally map-material" — this session adds to it, wrapping a little tissue paper around its stem, bristles still showing. Treatment came up Stained, already built into the same plan: the tissue's translucency behaves like residue rather than flat color, letting what's underneath show through. Opening came up Closed: the corridor's coverage should read complete and settled, a real event rather than another deferred pressure — any remaining incompleteness belongs to Inscription, not this base layer.

The corridor got a name: Dotti, found hidden inside "Ehdotti," a word from the AIKA book — a smaller word living inside a larger one, fitting for a corridor whose whole job is exposing what's hidden inside something older. Its scope grew too: rather than a narrow path to one edge, Dotti became a continuous arc across three consecutive edges — S, SW facing Mirror, and NW facing Shoreline — a corridor or meadow rather than one thin line. Structure and Application still hold; they just cover more ground than planned.

What got built didn't quite follow the plan. Hardening the tissue itself with CA glue was abandoned outright — brittle glue on thin, unbacked paper didn't work. Paper glue held the strips down instead, while the superglue stayed useful only for bonding strip edges to each other. Stained's quality came from a different technique: green marker scribbles went directly onto the surface first, then got partially covered by the tissue strips laid over afterward — a layered peek-through doing the same job the original plan reached for, just by a different material working better.

Dotti's boundary got a real drawn line in black pen, not just an implied zone, and a physical "Dotti" label was glued onto the tile, naming the corridor directly. New echo hexagons went in near the Shoreline-facing edge, drawn over the green paper and reinforcing the existing silver hexagon Shoreline had already pushed there in Session Six — though the metallic paint meant for them never arrived, so the silver treatment stays deferred. The tissue spilled further than required, too: strips physically extend onto both Mirror and Shoreline, not just the one neighbor Radiating demanded.

One thing wasn't planned at all: part of the "A" in AIKA's own anchor text got covered by Dotti's material, with a small yellow mark added where it used to be — extending AIKA's own yellow-and-orange residue language to a new instance of exactly that. Surface is complete, and Pressure's demand to reveal something didn't resolve cleanly: between the still-covered archaic text and now a covered piece of AIKA's own name, this session leans further toward covered than revealed, held as an open tension rather than forced into an answer it didn't earn. What's left for Inscription: mark the actual crossing at the edge (the tissue reaches Shoreline physically, but nothing's formally marked yet), decide what goes on the still-blank Provision marker, and give the echo hexagons their silver once the paint arrives.

## VI. Not Risen, But Something Just as Unsettled

*A word was proposed that would have named the old cost outright — awake, unpaid, plainly itself. What arrived instead was borrowed from a tongue that had never touched this ground before, meaning nothing here on purpose, and it fit the wound better for it than the honest word ever could have. Small green sprouts appeared where none were asked for, growing from material that was only ever meant to hide something, as if the hiding itself had started to live. And an old warning spread a little further, staying, for once, entirely inside its own skin — content to grow without needing to cross anywhere at all.*

Inscription reused the same six numbers one last time, mapped onto Scale, Form, Behavior, Relation, Force, and Residue. Scale came up Broad, Form came up Sign, Behavior came up Mutate, Relation came up Edge, Residue came up Mark. Force came up Propagating in its plain form — no 6 anywhere in this draw, so the Force Matrix never gets invoked.

Almost everything Surface left open, this draw resolves cleanly. Scale's Broad just confirms what's already true: Dotti already spans three edges, genuinely large. Relation's Edge names exactly what's still unfinished — the NW edge, where Dotti physically reaches Shoreline but nothing has been formally marked as crossing yet. Force's Propagating retroactively validates what Surface already did rather than asking for more: the effect was never confined to one bridge, spreading by route and bleed logic the way the tissue actually behaved, reaching both Mirror and Shoreline rather than staying confined to the one neighbor Cartography's Radiating obligation had named. Form's Sign becomes the vehicle for finally answering Edge: the moment to inscribe Attunement's still-blank Provision marker with an actual word and place it right at the crossing. Behavior's Mutate governs how that word should read — not settled the way Mesa's own inscription landed, but actively transforming, unstable, consistent with a Burial Cost that's been invoked rather than paid off. And Residue's Mark confirms the whole thing resolves into something modest and permanent: the inscribed marker itself, once placed, is the plain, small, lasting trace.

The proposed word was RISEN — the most literal possible answer to a Zombie-invoked Burial Cost: not paid, not settled, just awake and crossing, refusing Mutate's demand for finality by definition.

What actually got written was different: "Amedda," drawn from a Star Wars novel, chosen without any intended meaning attached. It still satisfies Form's Sign — a legible, discrete word — and arguably suits Mutate even better than the planned one would have: a name grafted on from entirely outside the tile's own vocabulary is its own kind of hybridization, unsettled by definition rather than made unsettled on purpose. It went exactly where planned, at Dotti's NW edge, the literal Shoreline crossing point.

Two more things happened that weren't planned at all. Small branching sprout-scribbles spread out from the green tissue strips, reading like little shoots growing from the green — unplanned, but perfectly consistent with Mutate's demand that nothing here be finished changing, and with Strange's material already feeling alive, wrapped as it is around a real brush. And AIKA's existing black warning-checker spread grew a little further toward its own eastern and north edges — negligibly, and importantly, entirely within AIKA itself. It doesn't cross into Ring or the Canyon. Nothing needs updating on either neighbor's own record.

Inscription is complete, and it closes the loop Cartography opened three phases ago: Activation invoked the Burial Cost, Map Effect demanded something radiate to Shoreline, and Inscription finally gives that radiation its actual, permanent, physical word — even if the word that arrived wasn't the one anyone expected to write.

## VII. A Name No Local Tongue Claims

*A small piece of the green was set aside and labeled, kept the way a keepsake is kept rather than a wound — proof, later, that something here truly happened. A word with no home in this ground was given a name of its own to carry forward, in case it should ever need to explain itself. Elsewhere in the deck, a small mistake was caught and corrected before it could calcify into a rule nobody meant to write. And when the record was finally checked against what had actually been touched, it turned out to have claimed two neighbors that, in truth, had felt nothing at all this time — quietly released from a debt they never owed.*

Chronicle reused the same six numbers one final time, mapped onto Record, Witness, Meaning, Publication, Maintenance, and Seed. Record came up Change list, recapping everything this session actually touched: the palimpsest renamed, Dotti built across three edges, Amedda inscribed at the crossing, the warning spread growing slightly but staying inside AIKA itself, and small tissue-strip bleeds reaching Mirror and Shoreline. One correction landed here too: an earlier draft of this session's own notes claimed Ring and the Canyon were affected as well. They weren't. Fixed before it could stand as fact.

Witness came up Archive object, and the object is exactly what it should be: a small offcut of the same green tissue paper used for Dotti, set aside and labeled for the material tray — done, as proposed.

Meaning came up Myth/rumor, and what the session actually produced is worth reading in full:

> *They say AIKA never truly buried what it swallowed. In the season the corridor opened — the one they call Dotti, a word hidden inside a longer one meaning "suggested" — a name arrived that belonged to no local tongue: Amedda. No elder claims it; no card explains it. The old sentries at the northern and eastern borders stirred at its arrival, testing their own edges as if remembering how to reach further, though they did not yet dare cross. Some say Amedda is a visitor's name, spoken by accident and now stuck like a splinter. Others say it is the name the buried thing chooses for itself, now that it has finally been given a mouth to speak with.*

Publication came up Website update: the T001 artifact page gets an S008 addendum reflecting the tile's current state rather than only its Session One birth, alongside a separate, direct update to the live site handled outside this draft. Maintenance came up Physical maintenance, and it ended up broader than proposed — rather than a targeted check of just the CA-glued strip edges and the "Dotti" label, a full clear coat went over all of this session's new material at once, protected in a single pass.

Seed came up Keyword, creating K035, Amedda — the imported, deliberately meaningless word now inscribed at Dotti's edge. Its return condition is exactly as open as the myth leaves it: if the name appears again anywhere on the map, or when AIKA or Shoreline is next targeted, decide whether it ever earns a real meaning or stays permanently foreign.

The Artifact Draw drew C016, with Brown 3 selecting Rule/documentation note straight off the main table. What got written was a genuinely useful correction: a new note in the rules' own Force section, "Confirming a Matrix trigger," addressing a mistake made earlier in this very session — a Brown of 5 briefly misread as calling the Force Matrix, when only a 6 ever does. The note states the rule plainly and gives the fix, so the same slip is easier to catch cleanly next time. Chronicle is complete for AIKA's first revisit: all six phases, plus the Artifact Draw, and a debt six sessions old finally has a name written where it can be seen.
