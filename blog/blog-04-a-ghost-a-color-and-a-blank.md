# A Ghost, a Color, and a Blank Waiting to Be Named

## I. What Attunement Prepares

*The court has spoken and the newborn thing is dismissed, but dismissal is not freedom. Before it may be touched, it must first be haunted, borrowed from, colored, and unsettled — not shaped yet, only made ready to be shaped. A trace is laid into it that it did not ask for. A logic not its own is set beside it to lean on. A hue is chosen that it will either wear or refuse. And somewhere in all of this, a small blank thing is prepared and left waiting, holding no name yet, only the promise that it will be asked to hold one.*

Attunement is the third phase, and it comes after the tile has been told what kind of thing it is but before anyone picks up a brush. Where Cartography decided T001's standing — bound, entangled, dense, proclaiming by mark under a burial cost — Attunement takes that standing and turns it into pressure: specific, binding conditions that Surface and Inscription will have to answer with actual material. It runs on the same six colored slots as before, only now each color asks a different kind of question: what returns, what material is called for, what reference the tile should lean on, what sign or color becomes binding, what direction the whole field must move in, and what small object should be prepared and held in reserve.

Attunement doesn't touch the tile physically. It also doesn't decide what everything means for good — some of what it prepares is deliberately left as pressure rather than resolution, so that Surface and Inscription still have real work left to do.

## II. What Returns, and What Must Still Be Found

*Something that was never placed is already present. It did not arrive with the session; it was already inside the wood before the first card fell, waiting for permission to be named rather than made. Beside it, a hunger opens for a thing that does not yet exist in this room at all — a future object, summoned before it can be held, called for the way a name is called before the person answers.*

Green came up `5` again — the same number that made Origin contagious back in Cartography — and in the Attunement table it reads as trace: choose one old mark, buried element, retired state, or palimpsest that has to stay influential. There's no literal old mark on a blank first tile, so the trace has to come from the cards instead: the robot ghost, read as a ghosted system, a remnant signal, or a mechanical afterimage that's already haunting T001 before anything's been drawn on it.

Blue also landed on `5`, and for Matter that reads as acquire: not a material I already have, but one I have to deliberately go find — a tool, paper, pigment, scrap, or object earmarked for a future session rather than this one. I haven't named it yet. That's the next concrete task on the list, and it's one of the few Attunement results that reaches past this session entirely.

## III. A Borrowed Shape and a Stolen Color

*A shape not its own is set beside it, so that it may steal structure the way a shadow steals a wall's edge. From outside, a color arrives too, and the newborn thing is not asked to wear it — only told that it now exists in relation to it, whether by taking it in or by turning visibly away.*

Red gave `3`, which for Mirror reads as structure: find a real-world map, diagram, floorplan, circuit, textile, manuscript, or aerial image, and let its underlying logic — not its literal picture — inform the tile. I haven't picked the specific reference yet, but I know the job it has to do: give T001 a borrowed organizing principle, something with its own relation, repetition, or division, rather than another picture to copy.

Yellow gave `2`, and for Omen that's hue: take one color from the card or sign and let it become a bias, an absence, or a contrast. The source here is the lion, and the open question is exactly what role its color takes — used outright, forbidden, drained out, or held as the thing the tile visibly avoids. That decision comes before any ground goes down, since it changes what "avoiding the color" would even mean physically.

## IV. The Field That Refuses to Sit Still

*Across the whole of it, nothing yet has decided what it wants to be. It is not empty and it is not settled — it is closer to a held argument, a thing about to become one shape or another and not yet committed to either. It cannot be smoothed over. It can only be changed into something else entirely, and even then, the court has not said what that something is.*

Brown gave `6`, sending Pressure into its own matrix, and the result came back as restlessness, at the scale of the whole field, with a rule of transfigure. That's a heavier instruction than most of the others: it's not asking Surface to calm the tile down or resolve it cleanly. It's saying the entire tile is currently unstable and unfinished, and whatever happens next has to change that instability into something else — a pattern, a structure, a symbol, a material behavior, a new meaning — rather than just filling it in or covering it up. Of everything Attunement handed down this session, this is the one obligation I don't yet know how to satisfy. I know what it's asking for. I don't yet know what shape the answer takes.

## V. A Blank Kept Ready

Black gave `1`, and for Provision that's mark: prepare one blank label, tag, notation slip, or symbol-holder and keep it available, without deciding yet what it will say. It's a small, almost administrative task compared to the rest of this session, but it matters — it's the physical object that Inscription will eventually need when Cartography's buried proclamation finally has to become an actual sign.

So here's where T001 stands now, all six slots answered: a trace from the robot ghost that has to keep haunting the tile, a material I still have to go out and find, a structural reference I still have to choose, a lion-colored decision about presence or absence, a restless whole-tile field that has to be transfigured rather than settled, and a blank mark sitting ready with no name on it yet. Surface is next, and it inherits all of this alongside what Cartography already demanded — the dense ground, the two edges held open, and now a hue to decide on and a restlessness to answer before a single layer goes down.
