# What the Map Will Remember

## I. What Chronicle Closes

*The court that opened this proceeding must also close it, and closing is its own kind of labor. Nothing more may be born or buried today. What remains is only the accounting: what happened, what must be kept, what may be told outward, what must be protected against time, and what small piece of this hour deserves to be carried into the next. The tile does not attend its own closing. It only waits to see what gets written down about it.*

Chronicle is the sixth and final phase of a session, and it's the one that doesn't touch the tile physically at all. Where every earlier phase added something — standing, pressure, ground, mark — Chronicle's job is to make the session recoverable: to decide what gets recorded, what gets preserved as an object, what story gets told about what happened, whether and how it gets shared outward, what needs physical upkeep, and what small piece of it gets planted for a future session to find again. It closes S001 entirely — the first full cycle, start to finish, for the map's first tile.

One thing to be upfront about, the same way I was with the Surface plan: what follows is Chronicle's *result*, not yet the record itself. I haven't taken the photos, updated the website, or protected the physical brush yet. This post is what Chronicle asked for. The doing comes after.

## II. What the Map Remembers, and What Gets Kept

*Two duties fell together this time: one to write the fact down, the other to keep a piece of the thing itself. The court does not trust memory alone, and it does not trust the object alone either. It wants both — a sentence that survives even if the object is lost, and an object that survives even if the sentence is misremembered.*

Record came back as affected map record: write down the wider map effects, not just what happened locally. The fact that matters most here is the edge condition — north and northeast marked as spread points, with the safety line now blocking the checker bleed at their shared corner. That's the piece of this session most likely to matter to a tile that doesn't exist yet.

Witness came back as archive object: preserve an actual physical or digital object from the session, not just a description of it. For T001, the object is the tile itself — particularly the fan-brush and the checkered edge — which means the concrete task is a full photo of the finished tile and a close-up specifically of AIKA, the safety line, and the north/northeast corner together. Neither of those photos exists yet.

## III. The Story This Session Leaves Behind

*Every finished hour deserves a telling, even a small one — not the record of what happened, but what it might mean if someone believed it enough. Time crawled up out from under what buried it. A tool gave up moving forever, and in exchange, left one visible mark of its last act. A line was drawn, and for now, something is stopped by it.*

Meaning came back as lore fragment: write a small piece of in-world meaning rather than a plain explanation. The direction is already implied by everything that's happened — time emerging from technical debris, a brush transfigured into permanent ground with one yellow-and-orange streak as its final gesture, and a safety line holding back whatever wants to spread at the tile's most charged corner. I haven't written the fragment itself yet, but the shape of it is already sitting right there in the physical facts.

## IV. Telling It Outward

Publication came back as website update: a brief entry rather than a long post — one image, T001's final state, its keywords, and the note about the blocked spread. That's a small, concrete task still ahead of me: nothing about T001 currently exists on the project website. This blog series has been carrying the story so far, but that's a different audience than whatever the eventual tile-index page will be.

## V. Protecting the Body

*What is fixed in place does not defend itself. The bond that holds a borrowed tool to its new ground can fail quietly, without announcement, unless someone checks it. The court does not ask for beauty here. It asks only that what was made be kept from breaking before its story is finished.*

Maintenance sent Brown into its own matrix, and — same caveat as always, since this part of the rules still has placeholder text — the provisional reading came back as physical map, repair/stabilize/protect, prevents future damage. In plain terms: check the epoxy bond holding the fan brush to the tile, and store T001 in a way that doesn't crush the raised brush or the built-up collage edges. This hasn't been done yet either. It's a small task, but not a trivial one — that brush is the one irreplaceable object on the tile.

One correction while I'm on the subject of protection: a coat of matt varnish went over the painted areas back during Surface, sealing the watercolor and marker work before any of the Inscription highlights went on top. I left that detail out of the Surface post entirely — a genuine oversight, not a late addition — and Chronicle's Maintenance result is as good a place as any to set the record straight, since it belongs to the same category of "protect what was made" that this result is already asking about.

## VI. A Name Kept for Later, and What's Still Ahead

Seed came back as keyword: create or hold onto a keyword that might return in a future session. The obvious, really the only, candidate is AIKA itself — time, emergence from under burial, and a refusal to be fully covered. It goes into the keyword list as something that might come back if it, or something like it, is drawn again.

So here's where things actually stand: Chronicle has told me what to record, what to preserve, what story to tell, how to share it, how to protect the tile physically, and what to seed forward — but none of the concrete actions are done yet. No photos taken. No website entry written. No epoxy bond checked. No keyword logged. The one thing that is settled is the plan itself, and the future obligation sitting underneath all of it: if a tile is ever created north or northeast of T001, the warning-checker spread points and the safety line have to be checked before any bleed or continuation is allowed to happen. The artifact draw stays dormant for now — that's a separate thread, for later. This post is the account of what S001's Chronicle asked for. The next one will be the account of it actually getting done.
