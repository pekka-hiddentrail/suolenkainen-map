# The Line That Holds

## I. What Inscription Asks

*The court's sentence, at last, is nearly served. What was proclaimed under weight may now be sharpened, but sharpened is not the same as released. A sign may stand taller. A border may be drawn firmer. But nothing here is asked to spread yet — only to make plain, in a single stroke, exactly how far it is allowed to go before something stops it.*

Inscription is the fifth phase, the one where specific marks, symbols, and lived detail usually get added to a tile. This time the result came back pointing somewhere narrower than usual: the sign already exists, and the phase's job is mostly to sharpen it and draw a line around what it's allowed to do next, rather than add anything large or new.

Worth saying plainly up front: I did very little physical work in this phase, and that was intentional. Surface already carried the heavy lifting for this tile — the collage, the buried brush, the checkered edges. Inscription's own result agreed with that instinct before I even picked up a marker: sharpen, block, regulate. Not build.

## II. A Sign That Insists a Little Louder

*The name that refused to stay under the weight of everything piled on it is not finished insisting. It wants its outline sharper still, not softer — a second breath drawn along the same shape, so that what was merely visible becomes undeniable.*

Scale came back as map-reaching and Form as sign, which together confirm that AIKA is still the tile's governing mark — the strongest sign already present doesn't get replaced by something new, it gets reinforced. What I actually did was small: highlights placed directly over the AIKA letters in black and orange, tightening the contrast against the schematics scraps underneath rather than changing the word's role. It's the same insistence from Surface, just given one more pass.

## III. The Bleed and the Line That Stops It

*Something at the border began to leak before it was told to. Black crept from the warning marks into the ground beside them, uninvited, the way water finds the smallest gap in anything meant to hold it. So a line was drawn — not to erase what had already leaked, but to make sure it went no further than it already had.*

Behavior came back as block, and Relation as edge — specifically, the rules flagged the option of using the line between the north and northeast edges as the active relation, rather than either edge alone. That's exactly where the actual mark-making happened. The black-and-yellow warning checkers on those two edges had already started to bleed slightly into the tile's ground on their own, black seeping a little past where the checker pattern was supposed to stop. Rather than clean that up or hide it, I added a single "safety line" right at the north-northeast corner, between AIKA and the spread points, specifically to stop the bleed from spreading any further than it already had.

That line is the block the phase asked for, made literal: not a wall around the whole edge, just a stop placed exactly where the leak was actually happening. The rest of the checker border stays exactly as marked — warning points, not yet open.

## IV. A Few Symbols, Kept Small

*Between the name and the line that holds it, a handful of older marks appeared — not loud enough to compete with either, just present, the way old writing is present at the edge of a page nobody bothered to translate. More sat quietly at the bottom, keeping their own company.*

I added a few archaic-looking symbols in two places: down in the bottom area of the tile, and in the gap between AIKA and the new safety line. Neither location was directly demanded by the Inscription result — the rules only asked for a blocking device and a sharpened sign — but the space between AIKA and the line felt like it wanted something in it once the line existed, and the bottom area had been sitting empty since Surface. I'm treating both placements as minor, almost marginal additions rather than a second governing sign. They sit alongside AIKA; they don't compete with it.

## V. What This Leaves

Residue came back clean, and for once that matches what actually happened: nothing here is left vague. AIKA is confirmed as the tile's one governing sign, sharpened rather than replaced. The checker bleed at the north-northeast corner is acknowledged and stopped with a deliberate line rather than smoothed over. A few small symbols sit nearby without asking for attention they haven't earned yet.

What's still genuinely open: the Force result landed in its own matrix, and that matrix still has placeholder text in the rules file, so I'm treating "region or shape propagation, possibly affecting two to six future tiles" as a provisional reading rather than a settled one — Chronicle should carry that forward as a documentation note, not a resolved fact. The Jules Verne sticker still hasn't been placed anywhere on the tile; it remains exactly where Surface left it, off to the side. And the north-northeast line is a stop, not a verdict — it says the spread is blocked *for now*, which means the map still owes T001 an answer for what happens the day a neighboring tile actually arrives at that corner.
