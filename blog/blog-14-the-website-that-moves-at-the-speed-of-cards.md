# The Website That Moves at the Speed of Cards

Every project like this eventually needs a home outside the physical board on the wall, so: suolenkainen.com exists now. Here's the story of why it looks the way it does, why it's built where it's built, and why it's taking forever in a way that, annoyingly, I've decided is actually fine.

## Cheating the Baseline

The honest reason I picked Wix: it's very good at getting an AI to slap together a generic baseline site in about ten minutes. Pick a template-ish vibe, describe the project, let it generate placeholder sections, stock imagery, a nav bar, the works. Then the actual plan kicks in — strip almost all of that AI-generated content right back out and replace it piece by piece with analogue material. Photos of the tiles. Scans of the cards. Actual handwriting instead of a generated font pairing.

It felt like a clever shortcut at the time: let the machine build the skeleton, then bury the machine's fingerprints under paint and paper, which is more or less the same move the map itself makes with the electrical diagrams and the technical scraps under a Surface layer. Get the structure up fast, then make it stop looking like what built it.

## Why Wix Specifically

Beyond the AI baseline trick, there's a boring but real reason: I already run a few other domains through Wix. Blog, basic ecommerce, the whole feature spread is already sitting there, already paid for, already familiar. Consolidating everything onto one platform instead of juggling three different site builders is just sensible account management. One login, one billing relationship, one set of quirks to learn instead of several.

That's still true. I stand by the logic. I'm just not sure anymore that Wix is the quirk-set I want to keep learning.

## The Part Where It Falls Apart

The performance has gotten close to unbearable. Adding a single element to a page — a text box, an image, anything — takes ten to twenty seconds before I'm actually able to edit it. Not ten to twenty seconds to load the page. Ten to twenty seconds after the element is already sitting there on screen, before the editor will let me touch it.

Text boxes are their own special case. Add one, and it appears — but I can't just start typing. I have to deselect it, then select it again, and only then does it let me edit the contents. One text block, start to finish, is somewhere around thirty seconds of fighting the interface before a single letter goes in. For a project that's eventually going to need a page-level presence for every tile, every session, every scrap of lore — that math gets ugly fast.

## One Page Per Everything

Which is the other structural problem, separate from the lag: this project needs a lot of pages. A tile needs one. A session needs one. A piece of lore needs one. That's not a Wix-specific complaint exactly — any site builder is going to feel this — but Wix's page creation flow makes each one feel like its own small chore rather than a quick, repeatable action. There's no real templating shortcut that makes "make another one of these, but for T003" fast.

So right now the honest state of the website is: behind. Not broken, not abandoned, just meaningfully behind the pace the physical map is setting.

## Making Peace With the Pace

Here's the thing I keep landing on, though: the map itself doesn't get built all at once either. Cartography hands out a tile's identity one card draw at a time. Surface builds up in layers because the rules say it has to. Inscription waits for its own turn. Nothing about this project has ever moved at the speed I'd choose if I were choosing freely — it moves at the speed the cards allow, and I signed up for that on purpose back at the very first session.

The website lagging behind the board isn't really a violation of that principle. It's just the same principle showing up somewhere I didn't expect it to. I can't have the physical map built entirely on its own unhurried, card-dictated schedule *and* have a website that instantly mirrors it in real time with zero friction. Eventually — genuinely, not as a hopeful platitude — the site will catch up: every tile photographed, every session logged, every lore fragment given its own page. That's part of the process too, same as the tiles are. I'm annoyed at Wix specifically. I'm not annoyed at the site being slow to arrive.

Whether Wix survives long-term as the platform is a separate, still-open question. But the "it takes time and that's fine" part — that one I'm actually at peace with.
