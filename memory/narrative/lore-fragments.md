## The Debt of Days

Status: provisional; written after S011 at the user's request; not confirmed as canon.

*It is said the Court keeps no clock, only a ledger, and that every day ever spent is entered in it twice: once where it was lived, and once where it was owed.*

Before the map there was one day, and the day was whole. The Ancients, who built nothing and found everything, were the first to notice that a whole day cannot be crossed — only stood upon. So they cut a Line through it, not to divide the day but to give it somewhere to be folded. West of the Line a day is a thing you are still inside. East of the Line it is a thing you have already paid.

The folded day is what the old texts call the Unburied Hour. It does not stay in the ledger. It surfaces wherever a cost was covered and never settled: under a name no one will claim, on a coast where two kinds of tomorrow meet. Those who keep watch at such places learn to answer the question the Court never asks aloud. Not *what time is it* — but *whose?*

The Line cannot be seen from either side. That is its only law, and the Court has never found a reason to break it.
